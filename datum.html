03

Eight shapes, one keeper.

Two-minute thumbnails before any CAD. Each one gets judged in a gloved hand first and on paper second. Seven got crossed out, and every one of them taught me something.

04

Sized for a gloved hand.

Smaller than a phone, thicker than a phone, and proportioned on φ. Every control sits where a thumb or index finger already rests.

A

72 × 116.5 mm, a golden rectangle. The screen is a square on top of a φ strip, so the lower strip holds the thumb zone.

B

Thumb reach, right hand, with a work glove on. Every on-screen action lives in the green arc. The measure trigger sits on the side, under the index finger, like a camera shutter.

C

Six layers. The sensors sit in the top edge of the frame so the laser and LiDAR stay aligned when it's dropped.

08

Decisions and their cost.

What I chose, and what it cost me.

Physical triggerPulling a trigger works with gloves, wet hands and eyes on the wall. It costs a moving part to seal against dust.
Small screen2.8 inches keeps it one-handed and pocketable. Reviewing a whole plan happens on the iPad instead.
Not a phoneNo apps, no notifications, battery that lasts a week. It costs carrying one more device, but it replaces four.
Thicker body14 mm fits a laser module and a real bumper. It's less sleek, and that's the point on a site.
What I would validate nextPaper and foam blocks in a gloved hand, a 3D-printed body with a real trigger, then a drop test from waist height.
