Encore

Every road trip writes its own playlist. Encore makes sure you get to keep it.

A music app feature that notices when a group listening session ends and turns every song you played into a saved, shareable playlist in one tap.

The problem

The best playlists get built live, one shouted request at a time. When the trip ends, the app forgets all of it.

The bet

Offer to save a session at the right moment, and people will keep it and play it again next trip.

The solution

Zero effort during the trip, one quiet card after it, and a playlist that comes back next time you get in the car.

Success looks like

25% of cards saved, and half of those replayed within 30 days. Pilot targets, not results.

01 · Context

Group listening is growing, and the car is where it happens.

Before designing anything, I checked if this was my road trip problem or a real one. The numbers said real, and they pointed straight at the car.

Why it matters beyond engagement

Music is one of our strongest memory triggers. In a UC Davis study, students recognized about 17 of 30 song clips, and about 13 of those brought back a specific memory (Janata, 2009). A trip playlist is a souvenir, and that shaped how Encore names, dresses and returns your sessions.

04 · Insights

The problem is not saving. It is timing.

1
Nobody curates while vibing

Singing, driving, talking. Tapping "add to playlist" 36 times is not happening.

2
Value shows up afterwards

You realize a session was special right when the app forgets it.

3
It is a memory, not a list

People describe these playlists as souvenirs. Name and cover matter.

4
The next trip starts cold

The real payoff is pressing play next time without asking.

How might we
help people keep the soundtrack of a moment, without asking them to do anything while it is happening?
05 · Exploration

Three directions, judged on effort in the moment.

The obvious answer was a "Save queue" button. I sketched it next to two alternatives and scored all three against the insights, not my taste.

06 · The hard part

Knowing when a session is over.

The screens were the easy part. One false prompt after a boring commute and people turn the feature off forever, so I designed the session model before any screens.

1 · Signals
Something ends

Car audio disconnects, a 30+ minute pause, or the phone leaves a speaker.

2 · Session engine
Clean the list

Keep play order, drop skips under 10 seconds, remove duplicates, include Jam and autoplay tracks.

3 · Worth asking?
Quality gate

10+ songs, not mostly one existing playlist, not a private session.

4 · Encore card
Ask once, quietly

A card on Home at next app open. Waits 7 days, then moves to session history.

Sessions that fail the gate still land in session history, so nothing is ever lost. The gate only decides whether we interrupt you.

07 · The full loop

From the last song to the next drive.

Encore is not one screen. It is a loop that only pays off when the trip comes back around.

If step 3 fails
Goes straight to session history

No interruption, and nothing is lost.

If step 4 is ignored
Moves to history after 7 days

After 3 ignored cards, Encore pauses and asks once if you still want it.

The calls I made, and what I gave up.

Minimum session10+ songsAny lengthProtects trust on short commutes. Small sessions still go to history.
Song orderAs playedRe-sorted by moodThe order is part of the memory. A smart reorder can come later as an option.

12 · Reflection

The smallest detail did the most work.

What surprised me

I expected the save button to be the hero. Walking through the flow, the auto-generated name carried more of the feeling than anything else. "Sedona Road Trip" turns a list into a memory, so naming moved into the first release.

What I would do differently

Bring data science in on day one. I sized the opportunity from public numbers. Real session logs would show the true share of 10+ song car sessions.

What is next

Run the ride-along interviews, prototype the card in a real car, and test two copy variants before writing production code.
